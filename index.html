What an FUE hair transplant can do for you

Follicular unit extraction gives you permanent hair restoration using hair that is already yours, with a recovery measured in days rather than weeks.

  • A hairline that looks like it was always there

    Each graft is set at the angle and direction your hair naturally grows, so the result reads as your own hair.

  • Wear your hair short again

    FUE leaves tiny scattered points instead of a linear scar, so shorter cuts stay an option.

  • Hair that keeps growing

    Donor follicles are resistant to the hormone behind pattern hair loss and keep growing in their new place.

  • Hairline, temples and crown in one plan

    Receding hairlines, thinning temples and crown loss can all be treated, planned around your future hair loss.

  • Back to normal life within days

    Most patients return to desk work within a few days, with no stitches to remove.

  • A donor area kept for the future

    Grafts are taken evenly and carefully so your donor area stays dense and future options stay open.

Why FUE is our technique of choice

Follicular unit extraction removes hair one follicular unit at a time, instead of cutting away a strip of scalp. That single difference is behind most of its advantages.

  1. Micro-punch

    A fine micro-punch circles a single follicular unit in the donor area.

  2. Extraction

    The graft is lifted out with micro-forceps, leaving a tiny opening that heals into a dot.

  3. Preservation

    Grafts are sorted by hair count and kept chilled until they are placed.

  4. Implantation

    Each graft is set with a DHI implanter pen at the angle your hair naturally grows.

Is an FUE hair transplant right for you?

Suitability depends on your hair loss pattern, your donor hair and your scalp health. You may be a good candidate if one of these sounds like you.

  • A receding hairline

    Your hairline has moved back at the front or the corners and you want it restored.

  • Thinning at the crown

    A widening patch at the back of your head that styling no longer hides.

  • Temple recession

    The corners of your forehead have deepened, changing the shape of your face.

  • Male pattern hair loss that has settled

    Your hair loss has been relatively stable, so a long-term plan can be made.

  • Good donor hair at the back and sides

    Enough healthy hair to move without thinning the donor area.

  • Female hair thinning

    Women with patterned thinning may be suitable once the cause has been diagnosed.

How an FUE hair transplant works

Follicular unit extraction moves hair from the back and sides of your scalp, where it is genetically resistant to DHT, to the areas that have thinned.

  1. Extraction

    Under local anaesthetic, Dr Khalil uses a fine motorised micro-punch to free individual follicular units, the natural groups of one to four hairs. Grafts are taken in a spread pattern so the donor area never looks overharvested.

  2. Preservation

    Each graft is checked under magnification and kept in a chilled storage solution, limiting time outside the body so the follicle stays healthy.

  3. Implantation with DHI

    Grafts are placed with Choi implanter pens. The pen creates the channel and implants the follicle in a single motion, skipping the separate slit stage and giving precise control of depth, angle and direction.

Does an FUE hair transplant hurt?

The only discomfort most patients notice is the local anaesthetic injections at the start. After that the scalp is numb for the procedure. Many patients watch films or listen to music, and any soreness afterwards is usually managed with simple painkillers.

How much time will I need off work?

Most people with desk jobs return after 3 to 5 days. If your work is physical or involves heavy lifting, plan for around 10 to 14 days. Dr Khalil will give you advice specific to your job.

Will people be able to tell I have had a hair transplant?

In the first ten days you will have small crusts and some redness. Once these settle, the transplanted area blends in. Because hairlines are designed to suit your age and face, the final result is meant to look like your own hair, not a transplant.

How many grafts will I need?

It depends on the size of the area, the density you want and the strength of your donor area. A frontal hairline often needs 1,500 to 2,500 grafts. Dr Khalil will confirm your number at consultation and never recommends more grafts than your donor area can safely give.

Are the results permanent?

Transplanted follicles come from an area resistant to pattern hair loss, so they normally keep growing for life. Your existing, non-transplanted hair can still thin over time, which is why we plan ahead and may suggest treatments to protect it.

What is the difference between FUE and DHI?

DHI is a technique within FUE. The grafts are extracted in the same way, then placed with a Choi implanter pen that creates the channel and implants the graft in one movement. Dr Khalil uses implanters to control the depth and angle of each graft.

Do I need to shave my head?

The donor area at the back and sides is usually trimmed short so follicles can be extracted precisely. The hair around it grows back quickly and covers the area within a few weeks.

Can women have an FUE hair transplant?

Yes, if the thinning is patterned and the donor area is strong. Female hair loss has several possible causes, so diagnosis always comes first. Some women are better suited to medical treatments such as PRP.

Why not have my hair transplant abroad?

Many patients compare UK and overseas prices. At Tajmeel, your surgery is performed by a GMC registered doctor in a CQC regulated clinic, and your follow-ups and any support you need are a short drive away rather than a flight.
